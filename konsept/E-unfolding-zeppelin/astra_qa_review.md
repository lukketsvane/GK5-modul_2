**Verdict: the bare assembly fits the hard size limits, and the nominal linkage geometry is correct. It is not production-ready.** The supplied validation reports 21 valid single-solid part definitions and no detected unmasked contact at nine poses. It does **not** establish that the assembled mechanism runs freely, holds position, maintains the specified clearances, or is safe to operate. This audit is based on the source and validation JSON; no STEP files or reference-render image were supplied for independent inspection.

**Envelope:** Yes. \(476.00\times165.4578\times200.00\) mm is inside \(485\times175\times300\) mm, with **9.00, 9.5422 and 100.00 mm of total dimensional margin**, respectively. That number is the union of conservative part boxes at the sampled poses, not the dimensions of a finished guarded product. The model **fails the separate 160 mm styling-depth target**. That failure is not solely a bounding-box artifact: the closed lower-crank tail reaches approximately \(y=-83.55\), and the mid-stroke hood reaches \(y=+79.73\), requiring at least **163.28 mm** of swept depth. Keep hard installation limits and styling targets as separate report gates; currently `within_box:false` only prints a warning and can coexist with `ok:true`.

### Ranked findings and minimum repairs

**1. Release blocker — travel and holding are imposed by software, not by hardware.**  
`motion(t)` stops at the intended angles; nothing in the assembly stops the physical cranks there. There is no spring, friction pack, hard stop, snubber, guard, or designated grip. The end links are exposed, and the rear tube sweeps across almost the full width. Two end covers alone would not establish safety along that tube. Neither the 12 mm trim gap nor the larger hood-to-leaf solid distance establishes finger safety.

The specified effort calculation also cannot be transferred unchanged. The modeled rear steel tube is about **0.142 kg** from its volume at ordinary steel density. Its centre height is \(138-26\sin\theta-25\cos\theta\) mm: it **descends** as the hood opens and contributes about **0.036 N·m of opening torque at mid-stroke**. The tailed lower cranks are not uniform two-ended links either.

**Repair:** fit the two adjustable, metal-backed stops to the **master only** and set them with the 12/40 mm gauges. Recalculate gravity torque from the actual moving masses and centres, then select and adjust the assist and brake. Provide a saddle-backed centre grip and positive support for servicing. Package transparent end guards and protection for the rear sweep; probe the crank, tube/post and travelling-aperture regions throughout travel. Measure centre and one-ended effort, skew, breakaway and drift, initially and after the specified 10,000 cycles.

**2. Release blocker — ideal one-DOF motion is correct; tolerance-compatible one-DOF motion is not established.**  
At either end, \(B-A=D-C=(0,0,22)\) and \(C-A=D-B=(0,45\cos\theta,45\sin\theta)\). This is a one-DOF parallelogram on the intended branch, away from toggle over the specified stroke. The cheek translates without pitching; the calculated rise is **28 mm**, the maximum intermediate walk is **2.23319 mm**, and the endpoint walk is zero.

The implemented cross-end drive is **not** the specified concentric shaft at \(D\). It is a **9 mm square tube welded between the offset \(E\) tails of the lower cranks**. If indexed and welded as described, it makes the two lower cranks a single swinging member; the two upper links are independent. The Ø18 carrier is a separate, nonrotating, 320 mm member with plugged ends. This is a coherent nominal architecture, not a missing second drive. But the welded lower member **and** the rigid cheek/carrier assembly tie the two ends together. Errors in ground-axis alignment, pivot spacing, weld index or span cannot be resolved by assigning both ends the same CAD angle. Neither the follower ground-eye compliance nor its axial float is modeled.

**Repair:** retain the rear \(E\) drive if that is the reference feature; revise the specification rather than adding a second shaft at \(D\). Fixture and inspect the welded crank pair for index and \(A\)-axis coaxiality after welding. Establish one axial datum, provide engineered follower float and in-plane accommodation, and jig the follower head at mid-stroke before locking it. Use the proposed ±0.75 mm axial and ±0.35 mm radial allowances as starting budgets, not proven values. Measure free-travel torque **before** adding the brake, then skew under one-ended operation.

**3. Release blocker — the pivot stack has zero nominal axial running allowance, and fastener retention is undefined.**  
The Ø5.98 journal in the Ø6.05 bush gives **0.07 mm nominal diametral clearance**, consistent with the radial target. Axially, however, the 6.00 mm shoulder spans **0.50 mm shim + 5.00 mm plate/bush + 0.50 mm shim = 6.00 mm**. There is no nominal allowance for thickness variation, finish or burrs. The Ø8 bush OD and arm bore are both nominally Ø8, with no specified retention fit.

The modeled receiving bores are also smooth: Ø5.00 for the simplified M5 pivot tips, Ø3.98 in the M4 post feet, and Ø2.98 at several M3 receiving locations. Simplifying threads in STEP is acceptable; **machining those diameters literally is not a tapping specification**. The rail screw has only **1.9 mm of aluminium web** available at its tapped location.

**Repair:** tolerance shoulder length, seated support face, bush length and shim stack together; select shims for a specified, measurable positive master running clearance and design the follower float separately. Specify bush retention and inspect the ID **after** installation. Add thread, depth, engagement and locking callouts. Use a boss, insert or captive nut where the available sheet/web engagement is insufficient for the qualified service load. No modeled helical threads are needed.

**4. Clearance failure — there are identifiable sub-2 mm gaps despite `failures:[]`.**

| Interface | Evidence from the source geometry | Minimum correction |
|---|---|---|
| Moving cheek return to fixed tray bridge | Closed at \(x=180,\ y=0\): return underside \(z=136-7=129\); bridge top \(z=127.5\). **1.50 mm local gap.** | Relieve the overlapping bridge or return by at least **0.50 mm plus** the tolerance/deflection allowance; preserve the load section. |
| Cheek return to hood tip | At \(x=219,\ y=0\), the cavity has ended but the hood continues. Its underside is at trim; the horn top is at trim − 1. **1.00 mm local gap** in every pose. | Relieve the horn top locally by **1.00 mm plus** allowance, or explicitly qualify a controlled co-moving interface against shell trim error and flex. |
| Rear square tube to post | At open, the rotated tube corner approaches the rear post face. Nominal separation is \(21.5\cos\alpha-29.5\sin\alpha-9\approx\mathbf{2.26}\) mm. | Only **0.26 mm** remains above the target before alignment error, weld distortion or deflection. The hood/leaf result is a genuine but narrower pass: the reported closed, middle and open OCC distances are **17.82, 28.24 and 42.11 mm**; the corresponding vertical trim-to-rim bounds are **12, 26 and 40 mm**. These are not whole-assembly minimum clearances.

`separated()` discards positive-gap pairs even when their gap is below 2 mm; the narrow phase flags only distance below \(10^{-5}\) mm. Named mates are skipped entirely. There is no common-volume test. The nine poses are **4.5316° apart**, not 0.25°. For this bare model the fastest link extremity is at most 52.3 mm from its rotation axis, so it can move about **2.07 mm from the nearest sample**. That is small enough not to overturn the hard *size* result, but too large to certify a 2 mm *pair clearance*.

**Repair:** expand broad-phase boxes by the required clearance and sampling allowance; record the closest part pair, witness points and angle. Use distance for separation and common volume for penetration. Check intended fits against their own gap/overlap limits rather than exempting entire pairs. Include exact endpoints, sample at ≤0.25°, and refine until the measured margin exceeds the pairwise \(v_{\rm rel}\Delta\theta/2\) bound. Repeat with hot dimensions, trim error, bearing play, weld error and load deflection.

**5. Safety unverified — no demonstrated stability margin.**  
The plinth is a solid **U-shaped** aluminium part, not a filled rectangular base or a ballast enclosure. Neither the proposed feet nor the ballast is present. At the open crown, a 5 N horizontal force produces **1.00 N·m** overturning moment. Even with a centred CG and feet at \(y=\pm51\) mm, about **2.0 kg total mass** is needed merely for static equality in the transverse direction, before a safety margin. Actual CG and foot reactions are unknown. The proposed 1.9 kg steel ballast itself requires about **242 cm³**; no such pocket is modeled.

**Repair:** add a mechanically retained low plate or ballast pan and the actual feet. Calculate the assembled CG and restoring moment to **each support edge** in both poses. Check sliding as well as tipping. Specify the incline acceptance angle, then perform the incline and separate all-direction 5 N tests with the production cord and feet.

**6. Safety and optical performance unverified — the modeled light engine differs from the specified one.**  
The model places a **central downward-facing 304 mm COB** in a 332 mm aluminium channel above the PC leaf. It does not contain the specified rear-edge white mixing cavity or baffled forward/downward task exit. A coloured `opal` solid is not evidence of a glowing dish, glare control or desk illuminance. At a nominal 24 V/8 W the current is about **0.33 A**; the real strip rating, thermal bond, driver, dimmer, harness, connector and strain relief are not defined by the solids. The drilled chase provides a possible route, not a protected wiring installation.

**Repair:** select the actual strip, thermal interface and suitably protected SELV supply. Detail grommets, strain relief, bend radii, post exit and keyed service connector; keep wiring off the moving assembly. Measure board, channel, PC and touch temperatures at maximum rated ambient and full power in both extreme poses against component and touch limits. Either restore the specified optical exit or qualify the central channel by measuring **≥300 lx average on the specified patch** and checking seated sightlines for direct LED images. Add an accessible dimmer independent of lift position.

**7. Structural and service assumptions — the shell is attached differently, and removal from below is not demonstrated.**  
The modeled hood load path is **shell → two bonded saddles at \(x=\pm124\) → carrier → screwed round spigots and cantilevered returns → cheeks → links → posts → base**. The fixed leaf is correctly outside that path. However, there are two saddles rather than three, no slotted tabs or compression sleeves, an exact saddle-to-cavity mating surface with no defined adhesive thickness, and no differential-expansion provision. One-ended user loading can load the shell bond in peel. The return sections, single radial spigot screws, post feet and bridge welds have geometry but no demonstrated load capacity.

The hood is an outer loft minus a smaller loft, not a verified constant-wall forming definition. The near-vertical trim tangencies and flat tip faces do not establish positive release draft. The PC leaf is modeled at **1.5 mm vertical thickness**, not the specified 1 mm. The rail screw heads face **upward** above the bridges, so the stated below-only rail removal is not supported by the fastener orientation.

**Repair:** retain the outer silhouette, but define the forming tool, draft, minimum formed wall, trim tolerance and rounded edge. Use one locating attachment and expansion-slotted, sleeved attachments elsewhere, or qualify a deliberately compliant adhesive joint with a controlled bond line. Carry grip loads into a saddle. Proof-load the returns, spigots, bonds, post feet and welds under asymmetric actuation. Specify the actual PC gauge and verify hot sag and retention at its two isolators. Reverse or relocate the rail fasteners for below access and demonstrate removal of the leaf, rail and connector with the hood mechanically supported.

**8. Deliverable qualification — neither STEP round-trip validity nor exact render fidelity was tested.**  
`validate()` examines in-memory B-reps **before export**. The three STEP assemblies are placed snapshots, not a solved mechanism. Reimport each exported assembly with an independent STEP/XDE reader; check transfer status, millimetre units, **63 located instances per pose**, part identities, transforms, solid validity, volumes and bounds. Repeat the critical clearance checks on the reimported shapes.

The source supports a continuous elliptical upper canopy, separate concave lower leaf, visible end links and rear torque tube. The lower portions of both posts are **raked**, not vertical, and the lofts have small flat end faces. If literal vertical stems are a reference requirement, change the stems while retaining the outboard head pivot coordinates and rerun the checks. **An exact visual match and luminous appearance cannot be certified without the reference image and a render or photograph of the actual revision.**

**Disposition:** accept the hard-envelope comparison and nominal pose geometry. Do not release the assembly for production or unguarded user trials on the strength of `ok:true`. The first physical build should be a guarded engineering prototype with corrected pivot stacks, physical travel restraint, qualified holding torque and the identified clearances repaired.
